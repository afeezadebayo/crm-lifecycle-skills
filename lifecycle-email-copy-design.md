# Lifecycle Email Copy and Design

A lifecycle email has seconds to earn attention in a crowded inbox, on a phone, often with images blocked or pre-loaded by privacy features. The best ones do one thing clearly: tell the reader why this message matters to them now, and what to do next. The worst look like miniature websites with five offers, three buttons and a paragraph of brand voice nobody reads.

This skill writes and reviews lifecycle emails (onboarding, post-purchase, retention, win-back, transactional with marketing elements and newsletters) for clarity, relevance, accessibility and rendering, and tests what matters.

## Operating principles
1. **One job per email.** One primary action, stated in the subject line and the button.
2. **Earn the open fairly.** Subject lines that mislead drive complaints, which hurt deliverability.
3. **Write for mobile first.** Most lifecycle email is read on phones; short paragraphs, large buttons, single column.
4. **Accessible by default.** Real text (not text in images), alt text, sufficient contrast, logical reading order.
5. **Personalise with data that helps,** not decoration ("Your order ships Tuesday" beats "Hi Sarah").

## Anatomy of a strong lifecycle email
| Element | Guidance |
|---|---|
| From name | Recognisable brand or a real person at the brand; consistent |
| Subject line | What's in it for the reader, specific, under about 50 characters as a guide for mobile |
| Preheader | Adds to the subject rather than repeating it |
| Opening line | The point of the email in one sentence |
| Body | Two or three short paragraphs or a short list; one idea |
| Primary button | Verb plus outcome ("Send your first invoice"), large tap area |
| Secondary link | Optional, quieter, for people not ready to act |
| Footer | Who you are, why they get this, preferences and unsubscribe, address where required |

## When to use, and when not to
Use it to write new lifecycle emails, rewrite underperforming flows, review templates, set an email style guide and design tests.

Don't use it for inbox placement problems (`email-deliverability`) or for deciding which journeys to build (`customer-journey-mapping`).

## Inputs to ask for, in one message
1. The email's place in the journey: trigger, audience, goal and what comes before and after.
2. Brand voice, spelling (British or American English) and design system.
3. Data available for personalisation.
4. Current version and results (clicks, conversions, unsubscribes, complaints).
5. Platform and template constraints (Klaviyo, Braze, Customer.io, HubSpot and so on).

## Workflow

### Step 1. Define the job
Write one sentence: "This email gets [audience] to [action] because [reason]." If you can't, the email isn't ready.

### Step 2. Write the subject and preheader
Draft five options across angles: benefit, curiosity grounded in content, urgency only when real, personal data, question. Pick two to test.

### Step 3. Write the body
Opening line with the point; supporting detail; one button. Cut anything that doesn't support the action.

### Step 4. Design for mobile and accessibility
Single column, 14 to 16 pixel minimum body text, buttons at least 44 pixels high, live text over images, alt text for meaningful images, colour contrast checked, dark-mode safe logos and colours.

### Step 5. Check rendering
Preview in the main clients your audience uses (Gmail, Apple Mail, Outlook, mobile apps) with a rendering tool or test sends. Check dark mode.

### Step 6. Check compliance
Clear sender identity, working unsubscribe, physical address where the law requires it, accurate claims, consent appropriate to the message type.

### Step 7. Test what matters
Test the offer, the message angle or the call to action, not trivial colour changes. Judge on clicks and conversions, not opens.

## Copy patterns by email type
| Type | Opening approach | Button |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding step | The outcome of the next step and how long it takes | Do the step |
| Post-purchase care | How to get the best result from what they bought | Read the guide |
| Replenishment | They're likely running low, with an easy reorder | Reorder |
| Win-back | What's new or improved since they left | See what's new |
| Re-permission | Ask plainly if they want to stay | Keep me subscribed |
| Review request | Ask for honest feedback, say how it's used | Write a review |

## Subject line checklist
- Says what's inside or why it matters.
- Uses real urgency only (an actual deadline).
- Avoids all caps, excessive punctuation and misleading "RE:" or "FWD:".
- Works with the preheader as a pair.
- Fits mobile previews.

## Email style guide essentials
| Area | Rule example |
|---|---|
| Voice | Warm, direct, no jargon; write like a helpful colleague |
| Spelling | British English; dates as 14 October 2026 |
| Subject lines | Sentence case; no emoji unless tested; no all caps |
| Buttons | Verb first, two to five words, one primary per email |
| Images | Live text for any message; alt text describes purpose |
| Personalisation | Always set fallbacks; never show raw tokens |
| Legal | Footer template with company details and preferences link |

## Personalisation that helps
| Data | Useful use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Last product bought | Care tips, compatible products, reorder timing | Recommending the same item they just bought |
| Usage data | "You've sent 12 invoices this month" with a next step | Surveillance-sounding detail |
| Location | Local delivery times, store events | Assuming language from location alone |
| Plan or tier | Features they have but haven't used | Upselling on every message |
| Preferences | Topics they chose | Ignoring what they opted out of |

## Testing ideas worth running
- Offer versus no offer in a win-back email (with a holdout).
- Benefit-led versus problem-led opening line.
- One button versus button plus secondary link.
- Personal sender (named person) versus brand sender for onboarding.
- Short versus long format for a newsletter.
Run each test long enough to reach enough conversions, and test one variable at a time.

## Edge cases and judgement calls
| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Brand wants long, story-led emails | Keep the story, but put the point and the button near the top |
| Image-heavy fashion or retail | Use live text for headlines and prices; alt text for every product image |
| Plain-text style from a person | Works well for onboarding and B2B; keep the brand in the from name |
| Several offers to promote | Split into separate sends or pick the most relevant per segment |
| Regulated claims (finance, health) | Get approvals; avoid guarantees; include required disclosures |

## Worked example
A subscription coffee brand's replenishment email had a 1.1% click rate. It opened with a brand story, showed six products and had three buttons. The rewrite used the subject "Running low on [blend name]?", a preheader with the reorder price, an opening line estimating when their bag would run out based on their order date, one button ("Reorder in one tap") and a secondary link to try something new. Click rate more than doubled and reorder revenue per send rose, with unsubscribes unchanged.

## Output format
```
# Email: <name>, <journey position>
Job: <audience> -> <action> because <reason>
Subject options (5) and recommended test pair
Preheader
Body copy (with personalisation tokens and fallbacks)
Button text and secondary link
Design notes (layout, images with alt text, dark mode)
Compliance check
Test plan and success metric
```

## Common mistakes
- Several competing calls to action.
- Important text inside images.
- Personalisation tokens without fallbacks.
- Judging success on opens.
- Clickbait subjects that raise complaints.

## Questions to ask before you write
- What single action should this email produce?
- What data can make it more relevant?
- Which email clients and devices does the audience use?

## Self-check before you answer
- Is there one clear job and one primary button?
- Is it readable and usable on mobile, with images off?
- Are accessibility and compliance checked?
- Is the test plan based on clicks or conversions?
